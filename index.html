// 01 · iOS & Mac · Swift · CoreML · Metal

My software journey started with apps for iPhone and Mac.

I shipped my first apps right after starting computer science in 2016. // 02 · Cloud infrastructure · KVM · Kubernetes

Today, I help build Europe's next-generation sovereign cloud at SAP.

After my PhD at TU Dresden, I joined SAP Cloud Infrastructure to work on CobaltCore, the new KVM stack taking over 20+ datacenters around the globe and more than 100,000 virtual machines. My Kubernetes components connect the OpenStack interface our customers use with IronCore, a cloud-native bare-metal operating system. I work on central pieces like the VM operator and the KVM node agent, and on Cortex, which you'll meet next.

// 03 · Scheduling · Go · Kubernetes

I'm the lead contributor of Cortex, the scheduler that decides where SAP's virtual machines run.

I built this Kubernetes operator from the ground up to make smart placement decisions for almost anything: OpenStack compute (Nova), storage (Cinder, Manila) and networking (Neutron), and even gang scheduling of GPU workloads on Kubernetes. I shaped its core: a knowledge database that ingests metrics and other data sources, and a scheduler built on Kubernetes-native inventory like VM and hypervisor CRDs. Today, Cortex gives SAP Cloud Infrastructure capabilities it never had before: automated failover partitioning and reservations, committed-resource scheduling, and capacity planning through an infrastructure dashboard.

// 04 · Agentic engineering

I'm always looking to improve the things around me. Lately, that means agents.

Trying new technologies and ideas is what I love doing, and recently I've focused on how agents can automate daily work and bridge knowledge gaps. Around CobaltCore, I've set up agentic pipelines for code review and release management, and for keeping our issue board and progress tracking current through a knowledge graph of issues and commits. Just as important is keeping them safe: a network-based sandbox guards against prompt injection and leaks of sensitive company data. I'm a coder at heart, and working with AI helps me grow and learn.

// 06 · Machine learning · TU Dresden

Classical and deep machine learning problems are my jam.

Ten years ago, I trained my first recurrent network for sequence prediction, and it failed miserably on Romeo and Juliet. I kept at it, picking up theory and practice along the way, from building balanced datasets to unsupervised learning. For my diploma and my PhD at TU Dresden (Dr.-Ing., 2024), I developed models for transport mode detection on phones and for error-robust, geometry-based traffic light matching, and published them at top international conferences.
